PREP DATA FOR AI (COPILOT)
==========================

ALREADY IN THE MODEL
--------------------

- Every table, column and measure has a business description, which Copilot reads.
- Technical keys, local-currency and cGBP building blocks, helper measures and both security mapping tables are hidden.
- Names are business-friendly and measures are grouped in display folders (1. Spend vs Budget … 6. Executive).
- Implicit measures are disabled, so Copilot has to use the governed measures.
- Q&A is enabled on the semantic model (definition.pbism → settings.qnaEnabled = true), a prerequisite for Copilot.
- Business synonyms are in the linguistic schema (culture en-GB), for example shelfware → Unused Licence Cost,
  spend per employee → IT Spend per FTE, constant currency variance → Underlying Variance,
  year-end forecast → Outturn, supplier → Vendor.

READY TO APPLY ONCE COPILOT IS AVAILABLE
----------------------------------------

The three settings below need Prep data for AI, which requires signing in to a Copilot-enabled
organisational tenant (Fabric or Premium capacity). This build was made without one, so they are drafted here,
ready to apply in Desktop (Home → Prep data for AI, about 10 minutes). They are saved on the semantic model
(the PBIP Copilot folder), not on the report.

2. AI instructions (paste as-is)
--------------------------------

----------------------------------------------------------------------
This model reports IT spend for a global organisation. The main user is the IT Finance Business Partner to the CIO.

Periods
- Fiscal years are UK fiscal years, April to March (FY2025/26 = April 2025 to March 2026). Data runs to March 2026. When no period is given, use FY2025/26.

Currency
- Money is in consolidated GBP (cGBP) unless the user asks for local currency. Actuals are translated at month-end rates; budget at the fiscal-year budget rate.
- Local currency only makes sense for one country at a time. If the user asks for local currency across several countries, answer in cGBP and say why.
- "Constant currency" or "at budget rates": use [Underlying Variance] for budget comparisons and [YoY Change % Constant FX] for growth. "At today's rates" means the Estimate rate scenario.

Budget and variance
- Positive variance = overspend (unfavourable). "Over budget" means Actual > Budget.
- When asked whether IT is on budget, give the reported variance [Variance vs Budget], the underlying variance at budget exchange rates [Underlying Variance], and the FX effect [FX Translation Impact], and say which one reflects management performance (underlying).
- For growth, give both [YoY Change %] (reported) and [YoY Change % Constant FX].
- Budget is not held by vendor: do not report budget or variance by vendor.

Efficiency
- "Spend per employee" is [IT Spend per FTE]: annualised IT spend per business FTE. Compare it with the benchmark band (£7,000 to £11,000 per FTE). Never sum FTE across months.
- "Shelfware", "unused" or "wasted" licences is [Unused Licence Cost]; utilisation is [Licence Utilisation %]. The best time to cut seats is the product's next renewal date.
- For delivery efficiency use CPI (earned value ÷ actual cost) and SPI (earned value ÷ planned value). CPI below 1 means over budget for the work done. Do not present cost per story point as an efficiency measure: story points are team-relative estimates and not comparable across teams.
- Exclude the project "Run costs (no project)" from project questions.

Actions
- "Where can we save" or "where should we act": use [Value at Stake], [Recoverable Value] and [Realisable in 12 Months] by Recommended Action, with the owner.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

3. Verified answers
-------------------

Select each visual, then … → Set verified answer, and add the trigger phrases.

  Page  |  Visual  |  Trigger phrases
  1. Overview  |  KPI tile Variance at budget FX  |  Are we on budget? · Is IT over budget? · What is the underlying variance?
  1. Overview  |  Budget to actual bridge  |  What is driving the variance? · Why are we over budget? · What is the FX impact?
  1. Overview  |  Spend vs budget by Department  |  Which department is over budget? · Spend vs budget by department
  1. Overview  |  Forecast year-end variance by month  |  Where will we land this year? · What is the year-end forecast?
  2. Spend & savings  |  Value at stake by action  |  Where can we save money? · What actions should we take? · How much can we recover?
  2. Spend & savings  |  Licence utilisation by product  |  Where are we wasting money on licences? · Which licences are unused? · What is shelfware?
  2. Spend & savings  |  Staff vs contractor variance by region  |  Why are contractors over budget? · Are we backfilling vacancies with contractors?
  3. Delivery  |  Project scorecard  |  Which projects are over-running? · Which project has the worst CPI? · What is the forecast overrun?
  3. Delivery  |  CPI vs cost per story point by project  |  Is cost per story point a good measure? · Which project is the most efficient?

4. Test, then save
------------------

Open the Copilot pane in Desktop and ask the trigger questions plus a few variations (for example "How much
is spent on shelfware in APAC?"). Check How Copilot arrived at this for the measures used. Save the
project. After publishing, mark the semantic model Approved for Copilot in the Power BI service
(semantic model settings).
